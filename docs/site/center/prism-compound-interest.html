Compound interest — the loop that pays itself

$1,000 at 5% for 10 years becomes $1,628.89 — $500.00 earned by the deposit, $128.89 earned by interest the interest already made. That second number is the whole idea. One worked decade first, every year on the page; the formula only after you have watched it happen.

Simple interest pays your deposit. Compound interest also pays every payment it already made — the machine feeds its output back into its own input, and that return hop, not the rate, is what bends the line.

What this page did not model

Inflation runs the same loop against your money's meaning: 5% nominal under 3% inflation is 1.05/1.03 − 1 = 1.94% real. Fees and tax are the other two counter-loops — on the gate register above, named rather than dressed in invented numbers.

1 · the deposit — early beats big

The seed works every round it exists, so WHEN it enters matters more than how big it is: $1,000 for 40 years ($7,040.00) beats $2,000 for 20 years ($5,306.60) by $1,733.40 — half the money wins because it ran twice the rounds, and the rounds live in the exponent.

2 · the slice — cut from NOW, not from the start

Simple interest cuts 5% of the original deposit forever: ten flat fifties, $500.00, a straight line. This machine cuts 5% of whatever the pot is now — $50.00 the first round, $77.57 the tenth. The difference between those two sentences is the entire subject.

3 · the pot — the only machine that remembers

Deposits and slices are events; the pot is state, and it hands machine 2 a bigger input every round. The frequency lever lives here: more rounds re-feed the pot sooner, but the ladder flattens — monthly is worth $18.12 a decade over yearly, daily $1.65 over monthly.
