+$100 a month — half the stream, half the ending

The deposit stream scales the curve linearly: +$100/mo on a $200 base adds half the base ending, +$121,997. It scales; it never bends — bending is the return hop's work. Next move: automate the raise you can afford; the recurrence does the rest.

+$10,000 to start — the head start compounds longest

A lump at month zero rides the growth factor alone: ×8.1165 over 360 months = +$81,165. Every dollar in it works all 360 rounds — the same dollar deposited at year 20 works only 120. Next move: windfalls go in whole, not in twelve pieces.

+1 point of rate — loud in ads, third of five here

7% → 8% adds $54,078 — real money, and still less than half the time lever. A point usually costs risk or fees to chase; a year of waiting costs $139,809 for free. Next move: compare this tile to the one above it before chasing yield.

compound daily instead — the myth lever, priced

Monthly → daily at the same 7% is worth $954 over 30 years; annual → monthly is the jump that matters, +$10,104. Past monthly, frequency is marketing — the exponent n barely moves once n ≥ 12, which is why banks print it in the largest font. Next move: read the rate, ignore the frequency.

the headline, eroded twice — the arithmetic

Tax first: 25% of each payment kept back runs the 7% engine at 5.25%, and the same 360 rounds land on $174,362, not $243,994. Prices second: 3%/yr divides by 1.03³⁰ = 2.43, so the taxed pot buys $71,835 of today's groceries — the real rate is 1.07/1.03 − 1 = 3.88%. Next move: plan against $71,835, and shelter the interest where the law allows — untaxed, the same account keeps $100,522 of buying power.
