Catan, read as a production floor

Eight machines turn two dice into a victory point. Every number on this page is from the base-game rules for 3–4 players, and the four states land on four things a Catan player already recognises — including the one that reports success over work it did not do.

the handleThe robber is not a bad hex. The hex still rolls, the board still says it produced, and nothing arrives — which is the difference between broken and not built, and the whole reason two states are not enough.

Read the line left to right, then down one row and left to right again — the break after machine 4 is drawn, not implied. The chip riding each belt is the thing that moved, named — and it is a different thing after every machine, which is the test a concept map fails: an arrow labelled produces never says what came out.

The four states, on four hexes you have actually seen

This is the part that does not survive a two-colour diagram. All four hexes below are not paying you. Each one needs a different move, and a red dot on all four would tell you to make the same move four times.

dice — 36 outcomes, and they are not flat

Two d6 give 36 ordered outcomes. 7 occurs on 6 of them; 6 and 8 on 5 each; 2 and 12 on 1 each. That distribution is the whole reason a 6-hex corner is worth fighting for and a 2-hex corner is a decoration.

number tokens — the board's lookup table

18 tokens sit on 19 hexes; the desert gets none. The token is what makes a hex addressable: without it the hex exists on the board and is unreachable by any roll, which is unpowered, not absent.

hand — the buffer, and the one with a cap

95 cards exist: 19 of each of the five resources. A hand is a buffer between production and building, and it is the only buffer in the game that is punished for being full — roll a 7 with more than 7 cards and half of them go back, rounded down.

trade — the ratio machine

Three rates, and which one you get is a property of where you built, not of what you hold: 4:1 with the bank always; 3:1 at a generic harbour; 2:1 at the matching special harbour. 9 harbours exist — 4 generic and 5 special.

build cost — the recipe card, and the reason this is a tree

Road: 1 brick + 1 lumber. Settlement: brick + lumber + wool + grain. City: 2 grain + 3 ore. Development card: ore + wool + grain. Read upward and the line is a dependency tree — a city is unreachable without an ore hex, whatever else you hold.

running — and the proof is a card in your hand

Two cities on a 9-ore hex pay 4 ore whenever a 9 comes up, which is 4/36 of rolls. Next move: nothing. This one works.

ghost — an honest absence, never a zero

The 5-wool hex pays every time a 5 is rolled. It pays someone else, because you own no corner on it. Reporting this as "0 wool income" hides the reason. Next move: build there, or accept you are trading for wool all game.

unpowered — it exists, and nothing can make it produce

The desert has no number token. A settlement on it is a real settlement, worth its victory point, and it will never pay a card from that side. Next move: stop waiting; the fix is elsewhere.

broken — it reports success over work it did not do

A 6-brick hex under the robber comes up on 5 of 36 rolls. The number is called, the hex is named, everyone hears production happen, and no card reaches you. A dashboard counting "rolls that matched my hexes" would show this hex as your best asset. Next move: move the robber — a knight, or your own next 7.

What this page was actually testing

Catan is not a pipeline in code and never was, which is the point: if the grammar only worked on our own build chains, it would be a habit rather than a skill. Eight machines, seven payload identities, four gates that decide rather than transform, and four states that each name a different next move — none of it needed the subject to be software.

Where it strains: the trade machine is not really a stage — it is a loop the player may run any number of times, and drawing it as one box in a line understates it. A belt-lanes rendering would show it as a lane that rejoins, at the cost of a busier page. That is a real limit of belt-line, stated rather than hidden.
