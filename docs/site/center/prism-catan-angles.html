Where this dissection strains

The census is judgment, not enumeration — the dev deck sits in chance and its 5 VP cards again in progression; another dissector merges them and counts 23. Mass has no unit — every rank is argued, and an invented mass figure would pass today's present-not-traceable gate, so the number is refused. 1 · subject intake — whole things take no questions

The box is on the table: a board, cards, dice, wooden pieces, one rulebook. Nothing can be explained yet because nothing is named — Catan is one word, and one word cannot be asked anything. This machine does nothing clever: it opens the box and starts naming. Beginning anywhere else means inheriting someone else's names without noticing.

2 · parts census — angle-blind on purpose

24 parts, counted before anyone knows who will ask. The order is the point: name parts for a known audience and you silently cut whatever that audience ignores — and every later seat inherits the cut. So the census names anything any seat could care about; ranking and cutting are machine 5's job. The proof it worked sits on the gravity map: 8 parts no seat pulls, kept anyway.

3 · sphere scan — the subject's own regions

The 24 parts cluster into five regions where parts act together: chance, geometry, economy, social, progression. No seat chose these — swap the entire audience and the spheres stay put. That stability is the test that you scanned the subject and not your reader: a "strategy sphere" would have been the strategist leaking into the subject.

4 · angle roster — a seat is a person and one question

Six seats sit down, each carrying one question it would pay to have answered. A seat is not a demographic; it is someone who could physically sit down tonight — "everyone" is not a seat. Each Catan seat names the seat it becomes on an application: end user, power user, admin, compliance, architect, investor. That mapping is why a board game is the rehearsal — same roster, different subject, tomorrow.

5 · gravity assignment — mass is how hard a question bends

For each seat, all 24 parts pull and five make the world, ranked by mass — argued, never numbered, because no unit measures mass and an invented figure would pass today's gates. The proof the seats are real is the mass test on the index: the robber read through six seats returns six different readings — a rule, a weapon, a dispute, a procedure, a valve, a story.

6 · page plan — a queue names what is missing

Each world names the page its question deserves: movement questions want a console, parts questions want an article, procedures may refuse the floor entirely. Six wanted, three built — The turn, revealed to be the new player's page built before anyone said whose it was, this method record, and The strategist, the first seat page off this queue.
