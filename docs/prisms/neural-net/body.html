The net stays greyed until the beat that introduces it, so you are always looking at the whole shape and never at a page that jumps.

1 The input is not an image any more

A 28×28 greyscale image is 784 numbers. Flattening throws away every fact about which pixel was next to which — the network is not given the grid, and it has to rediscover locality from the data or do without it. That single decision is why a plain MLP is worse at images than a convolutional net, and it is made here, at machine one, before any learning happens.

2 W1 is where almost the entire network lives

784 inputs × 128 hidden units = 100,352 weights, which is 98.6% of the 101,770 parameters in the whole model. Every intuition about "the network learning" is mostly an intuition about this one matrix. Each hidden unit holds 784 numbers — one opinion about every pixel — and the weighted sum is that opinion applied.

3 ReLU is what stops it collapsing into one matrix

Without a non-linearity, a stack of matrix multiplications is algebraically identical to a single matrix — 101,770 parameters would buy exactly the expressive power of 7,840. ReLU (max(0, x)) is the cheapest function that breaks that equivalence: it costs one comparison per unit, adds no parameters, and it is the reason depth buys anything at all.

4 W2 is small, and it is the part you can read

128 × 10 = 1,280 weights, 1.3% of the model. Each of the ten output rows is one class's answer to "which of the 128 features do I care about". This is the layer people inspect when they want to know what a network learned, precisely because it is small enough to look at.

5 Softmax turns scores into something you can bet on

Ten logits are unbounded scores; ten probabilities sum to 1. The conversion is not cosmetic — it is what makes the next machine's arithmetic mean anything. A confidence of 0.98 and a logit of 4.1 carry the same ranking and very different consequences when the loss is computed.

6 The loss is one number, and it indicts 101,770 of them

Cross-entropy compares the ten probabilities against the one correct label and produces a single scalar. This is the narrowest point on the whole line: everything the network did collapses into one figure, and everything it does next is derived from that figure. A pipeline with a one-number waist is unusual, and it is worth seeing drawn.

7 Backprop expands one number back into 101,770

The chain rule walks the same machines in reverse and asks each one: how much did you contribute to that scalar? The answer is one partial derivative per parameter — the payload goes from a single float to 101,770 of them in one machine. That expansion, not the multiplication, is the thing people find hard, and it is the reason this page ends on a console.

8 The optimizer is the only machine that writes

Every machine before this one reads and produces. This one mutates: each weight moves against its gradient by the learning rate, 0.01 here. Then the whole line runs again with a slightly different W1 — which is why the last hop on the console below points backwards.

Now watch one training step go round

Same eight machines, one loop. The chip is the payload: watch it stop being an image, become a distribution, collapse to a single number, and come back out as 101,770 corrections. The loop closes because machine 8 writes to machine 2.

What the console shows that the article could not: the payload's size does not travel with its importance. 784 floats in, 101,770 corrections out, and in between the entire network passes through a waist one number wide. Read as a still diagram, machines 6 and 7 are two boxes the same size as any other. Watched, they are obviously the strangest part of the line.

Where this rendering strains: backprop is drawn as one machine, and it is really the same four machines run in reverse. Drawing that honestly needs belt-lanes with a return lane, at the cost of a much busier console. Stated rather than hidden.
