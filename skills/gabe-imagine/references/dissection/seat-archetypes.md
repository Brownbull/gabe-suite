# Seats — the general rule, the derivation recipes, and the app exemplar

> Load when choosing WHOSE question a page answers, or when seating a fresh
> subject. The six-seat table below is the **app/product-shaped EXEMPLAR**
> (Catan rehearsed it; apps inherit it) — it is NOT the universal roster.
> The general rule and the per-shape recipes come first; icons are the
> suite-level set (inline Lucide strokes, `.gvi`).

## The general rule

A **seat** = a person who could actually show up + ONE question they would
pay to have answered + a refusal line (what they will not read). Seats are
people, never demographics; "everyone" is not a seat. The refusals are what
keep one page from trying to serve every reader.

## Deriving seats per subject shape

| Subject shape | Seat the people who… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| concept / phenomenon | bear its consequences, operate its levers, judge it | inflation: the wage earner · the saver · the central banker · the commercial bank · the economist |
| system / app | use, run, push, audit, design, fund it | the app exemplar table below |
| use case | do it · receive its output · clean up its failure | add-a-transaction: the one entering it · the one reading the ledger · the one fixing a wrong entry |
| code map | maintain it · call it · review it | the module owner · the caller-author · the reviewer |

A use case has FEW seats (often 2–3) — that is correct, not a thin roster.

## The app exemplar (the six-seat roster)

| archetype | icon | the question shape | refuses to read |
|---|---|---|---|
| end user | single user | what do I do, right now, without embarrassment? | theory, internals |
| admin / operator | user group | how do I run this for other people? | strategy — referee first |
| power user | target | where is the edge nobody computed? | recaps, flavour |
| compliance / security | scales | what exactly does the binding source permit? (only where a binding source exists) | analogies, anything not in the text |
| architect | drafting compass | why does this system hold together? | single-run anecdotes |
| investor / stakeholder | briefcase | why does this persist — who pays for it, what does it earn? | depth the decision doesn't need |

## The reading order — the general law

Order seats by **increasing distance from the doing**: the person inside the
moment first, the person judging the whole last. Instantiations: app —
use it → run it → master it → audit it → understand it → fund it;
phenomenon — who lives it → who operates it → who regulates it → who
theorizes it; code map — caller → maintainer → reviewer. The order IS the
taxonomy — never staple a second initial/advanced layer on top.

## Seating a new subject

1. Fill seats with people who could show up (name their moment: "first game
   tonight", "third audit this year").
2. One question per seat, in their words, + a refusal line.
3. Assign each seat its ranked gravity world (~5 bodies from the census).
4. **Run the mass test** — one high-pull body through every SEATED seat: as
   many distinct readings as seats, or the roster isn't real. (Six is the
   exemplar's ceiling, never a required count.)
5. Cap by usefulness and NAME the seat you cut, so the cut stays visible.

## A caller-named audience

When the context names the audience ("…for a saver"), that seat is seated
FIRST and the I2 defaults scope CRITICAL to it; the other seats still get
derived but enter the proposal as ghost queue entries, not as pages to build
now. The roster and mass test still run — one seat with no contrast is how
"everyone" sneaks back in.

## Page-kind tendencies (defaults, not laws)

doer/end-user → console (how does one moment move) · power/analyst → article
over the numbers · architect/theorist → console with a return hop (the
loops) · funder/judge → article (the story the numbers tell) · operator/
compliance → often PROSE (checklist, reference table) — expect the STOP to
fire, and let it.
